## SOP-MED-001: Mass Casualty Incident Triage
This SOP defines the protocols for managing a Mass Casualty Incident (MCI), defined as any event where the number of injured patients exceeds the immediate resources of the responding emergency medical services. The goal of MCI triage is to do the greatest good for the greatest number of people. The first arriving unit must immediately declare an MCI, establish incident command, and designate specific areas for Triage, Treatment, and Transport. 

Triage officers must rapidly move through the scene, spending no more than 30 to 60 seconds per patient, applying the START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment) method. Patients are categorized based solely on respiration, perfusion, and mental status. Red tags indicate immediate life-threatening injuries requiring rapid transport. Yellow tags are for serious but not immediately life-threatening injuries. Green tags are for minor injuries (the walking wounded), who should be directed to a specific holding area away from the main chaotic scene. Black tags are assigned to the deceased or those with non-survivable injuries given the current resources. Treatment officers receive patients from triage, perform necessary stabilization (e.g., tourniquets, airway adjuncts), and stage them for the Transport officer, who coordinates ambulance loading and destination hospital routing to prevent overwhelming a single facility.

## SOP-MED-002: Cardiac Emergency Response
This procedure dictates the critical, time-sensitive response to out-of-hospital cardiac arrests. Survival rates for cardiac arrest drop dramatically with every minute that defibrillation and CPR are delayed. Upon dispatch notification of a suspected cardiac arrest (e.g., patient is unresponsive and not breathing normally), the dispatcher must immediately instruct the caller to begin continuous, hands-only chest compressions. Simultaneously, Advanced Life Support (ALS) paramedic units and the nearest first responders (police or fire units equipped with AEDs) are dispatched at maximum priority.

Upon arrival, the first responders must immediately take over CPR, ensuring high-quality compressions with minimal interruptions. An Automated External Defibrillator (AED) or manual monitor must be attached instantly to analyze the heart rhythm. If a shockable rhythm (Ventricular Fibrillation or Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia) is detected, defibrillation must be administered immediately. ALS personnel will establish intravenous (IV) or intraosseous (IO) access to administer epinephrine and anti-arrhythmic medications according to ACLS guidelines. Advanced airway management is performed sequentially without interrupting compressions. Resuscitation efforts continue on-scene until Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC) is achieved, or until a physician orders termination of efforts based on established clinical protocols.

## SOP-MED-003: Trauma Stabilization and Transport
This SOP governs the field management and rapid transport of patients suffering from severe, multi-system trauma (e.g., resulting from high-speed collisions, falls from height, or penetrating injuries). In severe trauma, definitive care can only be provided by a surgeon in an operating room; therefore, on-scene time must be minimized to the absolute essentials. The critical concept is the "Golden Hour," emphasizing rapid transit to a designated Level I or Level II Trauma Center.

Upon scene arrival, EMS personnel must secure the area and immediately perform a primary survey using the X-ABCDE approach (eXanguinating hemorrhage, Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure). Massive external bleeding must be controlled instantly using tourniquets or hemostatic dressings. The cervical spine must be manually stabilized if a spinal injury is suspected. The airway is secured, and bilateral chest decompression is performed if a tension pneumothorax is identified. Once life-threatening issues are addressed, the patient is rapidly immobilized on a backboard (if indicated) and loaded into the ambulance. Secondary surveys and IV access should ideally be performed en route to the hospital to avoid delaying transport. The receiving trauma center must be notified early to activate their trauma surgical team prior to the patient's arrival.
