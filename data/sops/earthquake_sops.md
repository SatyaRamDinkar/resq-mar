## SOP-EQK-001: Building Collapse Search and Rescue
This procedure establishes the protocols for Urban Search and Rescue (USAR) operations following a catastrophic earthquake resulting in structural collapses. The primary objective is to locate, extricate, and stabilize individuals trapped within the rubble. Immediately following the seismic event, reconnaissance teams, heavily utilizing drones, must survey the affected area to identify collapsed structures and prioritize those most likely to contain survivors, such as schools, hospitals, and high-density residential buildings. 

Once a target building is identified, specialized USAR teams deploy to the site. The area must be secured, and strict safety perimeters established due to the extreme risk of secondary collapse. Structural engineers must evaluate the rubble pile to determine safe entry points and required shoring techniques. Search operations begin using technical search equipment (seismic listening devices, fiber-optic cameras) and search-and-rescue dogs to locate voids where survivors may be trapped. When a victim is located, heavy rescue technicians use breaching, breaking, and lifting tools to create a safe extraction pathway. Rescue operations are inherently slow and deliberate; safety of the rescue personnel is paramount. Extricated victims are immediately transferred to the on-site medical team for crush-syndrome evaluation and stabilization.

## SOP-EQK-002: Earthquake Medical Triage
This SOP outlines the mass casualty medical triage protocols necessary in the chaotic aftermath of a major earthquake. Earthquakes rapidly overwhelm local healthcare infrastructure, necessitating the establishment of decentralized field hospitals and stringent triage systems. Upon arrival at a disaster zone, the first medical responders must establish a secure Casualty Collection Point (CCP) in an open area, safely distant from damaged structures and potential falling hazards.

Medical personnel must utilize the START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment) algorithm to categorize victims rapidly into four color-coded groups: Red (Immediate/Critical), Yellow (Delayed/Urgent), Green (Minor/Walking Wounded), and Black (Deceased/Expectant). Treatment at the CCP is strictly limited to life-saving interventions, such as opening airways and controlling severe hemorrhage. A critical focus must be placed on identifying and managing "Crush Syndrome"—a deadly condition resulting from prolonged compression of muscle tissue. Victims trapped for extended periods must receive aggressive intravenous fluid resuscitation before the compressive load is removed to prevent renal failure and cardiac arrest. Transport officers must coordinate with the command center to allocate scarce ambulances and helicopters, ensuring that 'Red' category patients are evacuated to functioning trauma centers first.

## SOP-EQK-003: Aftershock Safety Protocol
This SOP dictates the immediate defensive actions and operational pauses required during and immediately following seismic aftershocks. Aftershocks are highly probable following a major earthquake and pose a severe, unpredictable threat to both survivors and rescue personnel operating in compromised environments. The command center must establish a dedicated seismological monitoring link to provide early warnings if possible.

Upon the commencement of an aftershock, an immediate, universally recognized evacuation signal (typically a continuous blast from air horns) must be sounded across all active operational sites. All rescue personnel must instantly cease operations, drop their tools, and evacuate the collapse zone along pre-designated, cleared escape routes to safe assembly areas. If evacuation is impossible, personnel must seek shelter under load-bearing structures or within reinforced voids. Once the tremors subside, a mandatory operational pause is enforced. Structural engineers and safety officers must completely reassess the structural integrity of the rubble pile and surrounding buildings before authorizing the resumption of search and rescue activities. The medical CCP and command posts must also be inspected to ensure their continued safety and operational viability.
