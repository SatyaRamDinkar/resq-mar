## SOP-FLD-001: Flood Evacuation - Residential Area
This Standard Operating Procedure outlines the protocols for executing an organized and safe evacuation of residential zones affected by severe flooding. The primary objective is to preserve life while minimizing panic and logistical bottlenecks. First, field responders must assess the current and projected water depths using hydrological forecasts. If water levels exceed 0.5 meters (approximately 1.6 feet) and are rising, immediate mandatory evacuation is triggered. Dispatchers must identify safe, elevated egress routes that are currently passable by high-clearance vehicles. If road access is entirely cut off, coordinate the deployment of shallow-water rescue boats or amphibious vehicles. 

During the evacuation, responders must prioritize highly vulnerable populations, including the elderly, individuals with mobility impairments, and children. Designated assembly points must be established on verified high ground, clear of potential mudslide zones or secondary hazards. Once evacuees are secured at the assembly point, transport them via heavy-duty trucks or buses to a pre-designated temporary shelter. The shelter must be equipped with adequate bedding, sanitation facilities, and emergency power. All evacuated sectors must be logged in the central dispatcher system to prevent redundant search efforts, and a final sweep should be conducted only if conditions remain safe for response personnel.

## SOP-FLD-002: Flood Rescue - Trapped Persons
This procedure governs the immediate search and rescue operations for individuals stranded or trapped by rapidly rising floodwaters. Time is the most critical factor, as structural integrity of submerged buildings degrades quickly. Upon receiving a distress signal indicating trapped persons, immediately deploy specialized swift-water rescue teams equipped with motorized inflatable boats. Simultaneously, launch unmanned aerial vehicles (drones) equipped with thermal imaging to conduct rapid aerial assessments. Drones are essential for identifying the exact locations of stranded individuals on rooftops or isolated patches of high ground, providing real-time situational awareness to the rescue boats.

Communication with trapped individuals must be established as quickly as possible, either via drone-mounted loudspeakers or direct radio contact, to provide reassurance and safety instructions. When physical rescue commences, responders must adhere strictly to triage protocols: prioritize children, the elderly, and those requiring immediate medical attention. Rescue boats must not be overloaded beyond their stated capacity, and life jackets must be provided to all victims before they are boarded. Once victims are safely extracted from the danger zone, they must be transported directly to a secure staging area where on-site medical triage can evaluate them for hypothermia, shock, or injuries sustained during the flood.

## SOP-FLD-003: Flood Medical Response
The Flood Medical Response SOP outlines the critical healthcare protocols required during and immediately following a flooding event. Floods introduce severe public health risks, primarily due to contaminated water, rapid spread of waterborne diseases, and physical trauma. Upon the establishment of a safe staging area or temporary shelter, emergency medical personnel must immediately set up a field medical station. This station serves as the primary triage and stabilization point for all evacuees and rescued individuals. 

The field station must be heavily stocked with essential medical supplies, particularly broad-spectrum antibiotics to treat waterborne infections, tetanus toxoid vaccines for puncture wounds, and copious amounts of clean, potable water to prevent dehydration and further contamination. Medical staff must actively monitor the displaced population for early signs of cholera, dysentery, typhoid, and leptospirosis. Any individuals exhibiting symptoms of severe hypothermia, critical trauma, or rapid-onset infectious disease must be stabilized and prioritized for immediate transfer to a permanent hospital facility. The medical dispatcher must coordinate these hospital transfers using high-clearance ambulances or, if roads are impassable, medical evacuation helicopters. All medical interventions and transfers must be rigorously documented to maintain an accurate epidemiological record of the crisis.
