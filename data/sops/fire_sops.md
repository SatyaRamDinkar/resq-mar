## SOP-FIR-001: Building Fire - Occupied Structure
This SOP details the rapid-response protocols for structural fires in occupied residential or commercial buildings. The absolute priority is human life safety, followed by fire containment and property preservation. Upon receiving an alarm for an occupied structure, dispatch immediately mobilizes structural firefighting units, including ladder trucks and emergency medical services (EMS). The first arriving officer assumes command and conducts a rapid 360-degree size-up of the structure to determine the fire's origin, spread direction, and structural integrity. 

Search and rescue operations must commence simultaneously with fire suppression efforts. Search teams must prioritize areas closest to the fire, above the fire, and primary egress routes. Forced entry protocols are authorized if doors are locked and individuals are suspected to be trapped. Fire suppression teams must deploy attack lines to confine the fire and protect the primary search teams. Ventilation must be carefully coordinated to release extreme heat and toxic smoke without inadvertently feeding oxygen to the fire. Once individuals are rescued, they are immediately handed over to EMS for triage and treatment. Firefighters must continue suppression until the fire is declared under control, followed by extensive overhaul operations to ensure all hidden embers are extinguished.

## SOP-FIR-002: Wildfire Containment
This procedure outlines the strategic approach to managing and containing rapidly spreading wildfires in the urban-wildland interface. Wildfire operations require extensive coordination across multiple agencies and resource types. Upon confirmation of a wildfire, the command center must immediately dispatch wildland fire crews, bulldozers for cutting firebreaks, and aerial support (water-dropping helicopters or fixed-wing retardant aircraft). Command must continuously monitor wind speed, direction, and humidity, as weather dictates wildfire behavior.

The primary strategic objective is to establish a perimeter around the active fire line. Ground crews must focus on flanking the fire—attacking the sides to pinch off the head of the blaze. Heavy machinery is deployed to construct wide containment lines stripped of all combustible vegetation. If the wildfire threatens populated areas, mandatory evacuation orders must be issued immediately through all available communication channels. Aerial drones should be utilized to track the fire's progression and spot secondary fires caused by flying embers. Structural protection task forces are deployed to the urban edge to defend homes. The operation transitions from active suppression to mop-up only when the entire perimeter is secured and no active flame is visible within 100 feet of the containment line.

## SOP-FIR-003: Fire Medical Response - Smoke Inhalation
This SOP governs the specific medical interventions required for victims suffering from smoke inhalation and thermal burns, which are the leading causes of mortality in fire incidents. EMS units responding to a fire must establish a medical triage area safely upwind and outside the fire's collapse zone. All rescued individuals must be immediately evaluated for airway patency, breathing difficulty, and circulation issues.

Smoke inhalation victims often suffer from carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide poisoning, even if no external burns are present. Therefore, high-flow, 100% supplemental oxygen must be administered immediately via a non-rebreather mask to any individual exposed to heavy smoke, regardless of their initial apparent condition. If the victim's airway is compromised by soot, swelling, or thermal burns to the face and neck, advanced airway management, including rapid sequence intubation, must be performed on-site by paramedics. For victims with thermal burns, the burned areas must be cooled with clean water or sterile saline, and covered with dry, sterile dressings to prevent infection and hypothermia. Critical patients, especially those with severe inhalation injuries or burns covering more than 20% of their body surface area, must be prioritized for rapid transport to a specialized burn center or trauma hospital.
